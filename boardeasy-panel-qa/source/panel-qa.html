1 The problem

1
What exactly is the problem you are solving? (likely)

Renting a boarding house in Naga City runs on scattered posts, notebooks and group chats. Students can't easily compare rooms, tenants sharing one meter can't check how their bill was split, receipts and deposit records get lost, parents can't see what's due, and landlords track everything by hand.

2
What evidence do you have that this is a real problem in Naga City? (likely)

Ateneo de Naga alone has around 7,600 students, yet for school year 2026–2027 it accredited only 19 boarding houses and dormitories, so most rooms students rent are on no official list. A city ordinance has required boarders to be registered since 1982 (Ordinance No. 1982-813), but there is still no shared digital way to do it.

Sources: Times Higher Education (enrollment); Ateneo de Naga, July 2026 (19 accredited); City of Naga ordinance. Open the links yourselves before the pitch. If asked about survey data, say honestly that you haven't run a survey yet, and that the pilot will collect it.

3
Why does this problem matter?

It affects money and safety. Unclear bills and lost deposit records lead to disputes between students and landlords, and families sending a child to a new city want to know the room is safe and the rent is paid. A boarding house is where students live for years, so small problems repeat every month.

4
Who suffers the most from this problem?

Students from outside Naga who rent for the first time, because they don't know the area or the landlords. Landlords also lose time chasing rent and settling bill arguments, and parents worry because they can't see anything.

2 The solution

5
Explain BoardEasy in one sentence. (likely)

BoardEasy is one app where students find and rent boarding-house rooms, landlords manage rent, bills and tenants, and parents follow payments with the student's permission.

6
What is your most important feature?

Transparent bill splitting. Every tenant's electricity or water share shows the meter photo, the readings, the real CASURECO II or Metro Naga Water District rate and the formula, and tenants can question any bill inside the app. That turns the most common boarding-house argument into a checkable calculation.

7
How does the app read the electricity or water meter? (likely)

Once a month the landlord photographs the meter. The app suggests the reading, the landlord confirms or types it, and BoardEasy calculates each tenant's share using either room submeters or one shared meter split by days occupied and declared appliances. In our prototype the photo reading is simulated; making it real is part of Phase 1. We use no hardware; it is software only.

8
What if the landlord enters a wrong or dishonest reading?

The meter photo is attached to the bill, so tenants can see the actual meter. The app refuses a reading lower than last month's, tenants can question any bill, and if the landlord corrects it, the old numbers stay in a visible change history. It can't stop every dishonest entry, but it makes them visible.

9
How does the parent feature work, and is it an invasion of privacy?

The student creates an invite code and chooses exactly what each parent can see: payments, the boarding house, safety notes or the Rental Passport. The student can revoke access anytime. Parents can also pay on the student's behalf. Nothing is shared without the student's approval.

10
What is the Rental Passport?

A record of a student's on-time rent payments, calculated automatically from their payment history, with a rating from "Excellent" to "Needs attention." Students can share it with future landlords to show they are reliable tenants. It is not a credit score.

11
Why include trash pickup schedules and Order Food?

They make the app useful every day, not only on rent day. Landlords post collection schedules and tenants get reminders and can report missed pickups. Order Food simply links to GrabFood or foodpanda; we don't process orders.

3 Technology and prototype

12
Is the app working, or is this just a design? (likely)

It's a working browser prototype you can open on a phone today. All four roles work end to end: searching, booking, approving, splitting bills, paying, issuing receipts and the parent view. It passes all 38 of its built-in automated checks. Payments, photo reading and GPS are simulated, and every simulated step is labeled on screen.

If possible, show the bill breakdown live.

13
What technology did you use?

The prototype is built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no framework, and hosted on GitHub Pages. We're developing it into an Android app and a web app with an encrypted cloud database and secure login.

14
Where is the data stored right now?

In the prototype, data is saved in the browser on each device, which is fine for a demo but not for real users. Phase 1 moves everything to an encrypted cloud database so landlords, students and parents share the same records securely.

15
Is the data in your demo real?

The places and rates are real: Naga City barangays, the four campuses, CASURECO II's ₱11.17 per kWh and Metro Naga Water District's rates. Rents are estimates from public listings. Every boarding house, person, review and payment in the demo is fictional.

16
Is there IoT or hardware involved?

No. BoardEasy is software only, which keeps it cheap to roll out: landlords don't need to install anything. The phone camera is the only "sensor."

4 Market and customers

17
Who are your customers? (likely)

It's a multi-sided platform. Students and boarders near the campuses are the main users, boarding-house landlords are the paying customers, and parents are a third group who use it free. Schools and the city government benefit from safer, better-documented student housing.

18
How big is your market?

We start with the area around CSNHS, Ateneo de Naga and UNC, where Ateneo alone has around 7,600 students, then expand to all 27 barangays. We don't yet have a verified count of every boarding house in Naga; the city's business permits office holds that data, and getting it is part of our LGU partnership.

Don't invent a market-size figure. Admitting the gap and naming where the data lives sounds more credible.

19
Why start in Naga City?

It's our home city and a university city with several campuses close together, so a small pilot reaches many students and landlords quickly. The app is already built around Naga's real barangays and utility rates.

5 Business model and pricing

20
How will BoardEasy make money? (likely)

We're a multi-sided platform with a freemium model: free for students and parents. Landlords pay a subscription, estimated at ₱25 per room per month. We'd add a small fee, estimated at ₱5 per in-app payment, and referral fees from local service partners. These are starting assumptions we'll test in the pilot.

21
Why would landlords pay for it? (likely)

It saves them time chasing rent, cuts arguments over bills and deposits, and fills rooms faster. At ₱25 a room it's cheap: a four-room house pays ₱100 a month, about the rent of one ₱3,000 room for a single day. If the app fills a vacancy one day sooner, it has paid for itself.

22
Why not charge students instead?

Students have limited budgets, and free access brings them onto the platform. A landlord gains value from every student who uses the app, so charging the landlord side is fairer and makes growth easier.

23
When will you start earning?

Not during the pilot; it's free so landlords can try it. After the pilot we'd start charging landlords who found it useful. Early revenue would be small and cover running costs; sustainability comes from signing up many boarding houses across the city.

24
What happens if landlords refuse to pay?

Then we learn that in the pilot, before spending more. We'd adjust the price, offer a basic free tier with paid extras, or rely more on transaction and referral fees. Testing willingness to pay is exactly why the pilot comes first.

6 Competition

25
Who are your competitors, and what makes you different? (likely)

Finder apps like GrabHostel and Lookal help students find rooms; landlord tools like Renthuzz and Rentmo help collect rent; many people just use Facebook groups. Other apps help you find a room or help landlords collect rent. BoardEasy does both, adds parents, and shows the math behind every shared bill.

26
Why wouldn't people just keep using Facebook?

Facebook is good for posting, but it doesn't track rent, split bills, keep receipts, record deposits or let parents follow payments. A landlord would still need notebooks and chat threads; BoardEasy replaces those.

27
What stops a bigger company from copying you?

Nothing stops copying the idea, but our advantage is local: real Naga rates, relationships with landlords near the campuses, and an LGU pilot. Being first in Naga with verified listings and landlords already onboarded is hard to copy quickly.

7 Budget and funding

28
How much are you asking for, and what is it for? (likely)

₱35,000. ₱12,000 for app and cloud setup, including the Android app on Google Play and secure login; ₱10,500 for site inspections, QR room tags and onboarding 25 pilot boarding houses; ₱6,000 for a Data Privacy Act review; and ₱6,500 for operations and contingency during the three-month pilot.

29
How did you arrive at these amounts?

They are estimates for a lean, software-only pilot. Hosting can start on free cloud tiers, Google Play registration is a one-time US$25, and most of the budget goes to the pilot itself: visiting, verifying and onboarding 25 boarding houses.

Be ready to explain each line. Confirm local prices (printing, transport) before presenting.

30
Why spend ₱6,000 on data privacy?

We handle names, contact details, payments and room photos, and some users are minors. The Data Privacy Act requires us to protect that data properly, so a review before real users join is necessary, not optional.

31
What if you get less money than you asked for?

We'd shrink the pilot rather than skip steps: fewer boarding houses, and the data privacy review stays. Even a smaller pilot would test whether landlords use and value the app.

8 Implementation and pilot

32
What is your timeline? (likely)

Twelve months. Months 1 to 3: move to an encrypted cloud database with secure login and launch an Android beta. Months 4 to 6: pilot with 25 boarding houses, about 150 rooms, near CSNHS, Ateneo de Naga and UNC. Months 7 to 12: work with the city's business permits and solid waste offices to expand across all 27 barangays.

33
How will you measure if the pilot is successful?

We'll track the number of bill disputes, on-time payment rates, waste-pickup reports, and how many landlords keep using the app and would pay for it after the pilot. The app already records most of this automatically.

34
How will you convince 25 landlords to join?

By visiting them in person, offering the app free during the pilot, helping them set up their rooms and tenants, and giving each room a QR tag. We'll start with boarding houses near the campuses, where students are already looking.

35
How will payments work during the pilot?

Payments made outside the app, in cash, GCash or Maya, are recorded in the app by the landlord, with receipts. A licensed payment gateway comes later, once the business is registered.

9 Partners and the LGU

36
Do you already have partners? (likely)

Not yet; none is formalized. We're approaching the Naga City LGU for the pilot and official data, DOST for technology support, a TBI incubator for mentoring and seed funding, and the campuses to reach incoming students.

Never imply a partnership exists before it is signed.

37
What do you want from the LGU specifically?

Approval to run the pilot, help reaching boarding-house owners through the business permits office, and official data such as waste collection schedules and flood hazard maps. In return, the city gets better records of student housing, which supports its boarder registration ordinance.

Confirm the exact office names (BPLO and SWMO) before presenting.

10 Privacy and security

38
How will you protect users' personal data? (likely)

Phase 1 adds an encrypted cloud database and secure OTP login, and part of our budget pays for a Data Privacy Act compliance review. Users only see what their role allows, and parents see only what the student permits.

39
Does the app track students' locations?

No. The distance feature in the prototype is simulated and never reads the phone's real location. In a future version, location would only be used with the student's consent, sharing is off by default, it shares distance only (not a map position), and it expires automatically.

40
How do you make sure listings and reviews are genuine?

Listings are reviewed by an administrator before being marked verified, and the pilot adds in-person site inspections. Reviews only carry a "verified tenant" label after a recorded stay, and anyone can report a misleading listing or review.

11 Risks and limitations

41
What is the biggest risk to your startup?

Landlord adoption. If landlords don't use the app, students won't find rooms on it. That's why the pilot is free, done in person, and focused on the area near the campuses, so we can prove value before charging.

42
What if landlords or tenants aren't good with technology?

We onboard landlords in person, and the dashboard shows what needs attention today. Landlords can also track tenants who don't have the app, so nobody is left out.

43
What are the current limitations of your prototype?

Payments, meter photo reading, GPS and the verified badge are simulated; data is stored only on one device; and all records are demo data. Our Phase 1 plan addresses each of these.

44
You are high school students. Can you really run this?

We've already built a working prototype with four user roles, which shows we can build. For the next stage we're asking for mentoring through a TBI incubator and starting with a small, manageable pilot before scaling.

What social impact will BoardEasy have?

Safer, fairer and better-documented student housing. Students get transparent bills and protected deposits, families get peace of mind, landlords get organized records, and the city gets better information about where students live. This aligns with the goal of safe and affordable housing in the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 11).

47
Where do you see BoardEasy in a few years?

First, the trusted student-housing app across Naga City's 27 barangays. If that works, the same model could serve other university cities, but we'll prove it in Naga first.

48
If you could only keep one feature, which would it be?

Transparent bill splitting, because it solves a monthly problem for both tenants and landlords and is what makes BoardEasy different from listing apps.
