020:20 to 1:0040 seconds

Problem statement

"Naga City is a university city. Ateneo de Naga University alone has around 7,600 students, yet for school year 2026–2027 its housing office accredited only 19 boarding houses and dormitories. Most rooms students rent fall outside any official list. A city ordinance has required boarders to be registered since 1982, but there is still no shared, digital way to do it.

So the process runs on scattered posts, notebooks and group chats. Students can't easily compare rent, rules or safety. When tenants share one electricity meter, nobody can check how the bill was split. Receipts and deposit records get lost. Parents can't see what's due, and landlords track it all by hand."

Sources: Times Higher Education, Ateneo de Naga University profile (about 7,600 students); Ateneo de Naga University, "ADNU Fosters Safe, Supportive Spaces through ABHD Service," July 2026 (19 accredited for SY 2026–2027); City of Naga Ordinance No. 1982-813 (boarder registration). Put these on the slide in small print.

031:00 to 1:3030 seconds

Solution

"BoardEasy puts that whole cycle in one app. Students search, compare and book rooms. Landlords manage rooms, tenants, rent and deposits. Monthly bills are split by a formula everyone can see. And parents follow payments only with the student's permission. One shared record replaces guesswork."

041:30 to 2:1545 seconds

Innovation and features

"Three features set us apart. First, transparent bill splitting. Each tenant's electricity or water share shows the meter readings, the real CASURECO II or Metro Naga Water District rate, and the formula. Tenants can question any bill inside the app.

Second, permission-based parent access. The student creates an invite code and decides exactly what each parent sees. Third, the Rental Passport: on-time payments become a record a student can show a future landlord.

On the tech side, today's prototype runs in the browser, built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript. We're developing it into an Android app and a web app. Other apps either list rooms or help landlords collect rent. BoardEasy does both, and adds parents."

BoardEasy is software only. Leave out the template's IoT and ESP32 examples.

Target market

"We serve a multi-sided market in Naga City. Students and boarders near the four campuses are our users. Boarding-house owners, from bedspaces to dormitories, are our paying customers. Parents and guardians are a key third group. We also work with schools' student affairs offices and the city government, who care about safe, fairly priced student housing."

062:45 to 3:1530 seconds

Business model

"Following Harvard Business School's business model types, BoardEasy is a multi-sided platform with a freemium approach. Students and parents use it free. Landlords pay a subscription: our working estimate is ₱25 per room per month. We'd add a small transaction fee, estimated at ₱5 per in-app payment, and referral fees from local service partners. These are starting assumptions, and we'll validate them with Naga landlords during the pilot."

Asking / proposed fund

"We're requesting a ₱35,000 seed grant for app launch and a campus pilot. ₱12,000, or 34%, for app and cloud setup: a cloud database, our Android app on Google Play, and secure OTP login. ₱10,500, or 30%, for field verification: site inspections, QR room tags and onboarding for 25 pilot boarding houses. ₱6,000, or 17%, for a Data Privacy Act review, since some student users are minors, plus standard lease templates. And ₱6,500, or 19%, for operations and contingency during the three-month pilot."

Estimated amounts. Payments stay manually recorded during the pilot; a licensed gateway comes in Phase 3, once the business is registered.

Implementation strategy

"Our roadmap runs 12 months. Phase 1, months 1 to 3: backend, security and Android app. We move from browser storage to an encrypted cloud database, with OTP login, meter reading and an Android beta. Phase 2, months 4 to 6: university-belt pilot. We onboard 25 boarding houses, about 150 rooms, near CSNHS, Ateneo de Naga and UNC, and measure bill disputes, on-time payments and waste-pickup reports. Phase 3, months 7 to 12: citywide scaling. We approach the city's business permits and solid waste offices to expand across all 27 barangays, and add a licensed payment gateway."

Confirm the exact names of the two city offices (BPLO and SWMO) before presenting.
